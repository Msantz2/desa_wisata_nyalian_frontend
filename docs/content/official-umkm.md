## 1. Capil Craft — Anyaman Capil (Woven Coconut-Leaf Hat)

**Location:** Banjar/Dusun Pemenang, Desa Adat Pemenang, Nyalian Village
**Category:** Traditional handicraft, hereditary household industry

### Description
Capil is a traditional wide-brimmed hat woven from young coconut leaf (janur), historically worn as everyday protective headwear for farming and outdoor work across Bali. In Nyalian, capil-weaving is concentrated in Dusun Pemenang, where it has been practiced as a hereditary craft passed down through generations of local families — village youth describe it as a livelihood found in this banjar and not replicated elsewhere within Nyalian itself.

### Material & Technique
- **Primary material:** Young coconut leaf (janur), locally referred to by some village sources as *demela* when still young/unopened
- **Alternative/lower-cost material:** Slepan (mature coconut leaf), which produces a less refined but cheaper hat
- **Unit conversion:** One ikat (bundle of 50 janur stems) can be woven into one finished capil
- **Weather sensitivity:** Janur is highly perishable and prone to developing mold (dark spotting) during the rainy season, directly affecting both supply and finished-product quality

### Artisans
Documented capil weavers in Dusun Pemenang include **Ni Wayan Remi** (also referenced in reporting as Ni Nyoman Remi — spelling varies across sources; a 50-year-old mother of three) and **Nengah Suda**, among other members of the local weaving community. **[Confirm with Desa Adat Pemenang / Pokdarwis Nyalian]** the current, complete list of active artisans before publishing individual names on the site, and obtain consent from each named artisan.

### Economic Profile
- **Retail price per hat:** Rp 15,000 (slepan/lower-grade material) to Rp 25,000 (clean, high-quality janur), as of reporting from 2022
- **Production rate:** An experienced weaver can typically complete 10–15 capil per day
- **Sales channel:** Primarily sold through pengepul (wholesale collectors) based in Tabanan and Gianyar, rather than direct-to-consumer
- **Margins:** Reported as thin — profit of roughly Rp 25,000+ per kodi (a unit of 20 hats), meaning individual artisan margins are modest and highly sensitive to raw material cost
- **Raw material sourcing challenge:** During shortages, artisans have had to source janur from Gianyar or even from outside Bali (Lombok, Java), where imported leaf is shorter and considered lower quality than local janur

*(Note: the figures above reflect wholesale/retail hat pricing, not a visitor workshop fee — see cross-reference to Tour Package 5 below.)*

### Development & Recognition
- In August 2019, a community-service program (Program Kemitraan Masyarakat Stimulus) by STIE Satya Dharma Singaraja — funded via a national research and higher-education grant (DRPM Ristekdikti) — delivered training to Dusun Pemenang's capil weavers, covering marketing, financial management, entrepreneurship, product innovation, and basic English (the latter specifically aimed at tourism readiness).
- In June 2023, Nyalian's village government (Perbekel) hosted a joint visit from a national ministry-level team to the Dusun Pemenang capil artisans, indicating a degree of formal government recognition of the craft's potential.
- Local youth-led advocacy (documented via a BASAbaliWiki community submission) has called for the craft to be formally recognized as a provincial-level UMKM and elevated as a village icon (Ikon Desa), citing weak promotion/marketing as a key barrier to be addressed.

### Challenges (for honest, non-romanticized representation)
- Raw material scarcity and quality inconsistency, especially in the rainy season
- Thin profit margins relative to labor input
- Limited promotion and market access beyond existing wholesale relationships
- Competition from capil production in other regencies, meaning Nyalian's version is not unique in Bali overall — its distinction is being *the* hereditary craft of this specific banjar, not being the only place in Bali that makes capil

### Visitor Experience Potential
This craft is already built into `official-tour-packages.md` as **Package 5: Village Life & Craft Immersion**, which includes an artisan-led demonstration, a hands-on mini-workshop, and a take-home capil hat. To make this genuinely beneficial rather than extractive:
- Workshop pricing should be set separately from wholesale hat pricing above, and should ensure a fair, direct payment to the participating artisan(s) — not merely covering the cost of one Rp 15,000–25,000 hat
- Framing should be honest about the craft's real economic pressures (per the Challenges section) rather than presenting it as purely quaint or nostalgic
